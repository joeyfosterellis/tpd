# tpd

The simplest way to explain the equation to someone seeing it cold:

$$
0 \xrightarrow{T_1} 1 \xrightarrow{T_2} 2 \xrightarrow{T_3} 3 \xrightarrow{T_4} 4 \xrightarrow{T_5} 5 \xrightarrow{T_6} 6 \xrightarrow{T_7} 7 \xrightarrow{T_0} 0'
\qquad 0' \neq 0
$$

## The states: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

These are not meanings. They are simply different observable states. "7" is no more special than "3."

## The arrows are the important part

Each arrow $T$ represents whatever causes one state to become another: heat, breath, pressure, movement, time, language, learning, decay, experience, etc.

**So the theory studies transformation, not the numbers.**

For example:

$$
3 \xrightarrow{T_4} 4
$$

asks:

> What had to happen for state 3 to become state 4?

And:

$$
4 \xrightarrow{} 3
$$

raises a second question:

> Can the transformation actually be reversed?

Sometimes yes. Sometimes no.

## The final $0'$ is the key

The system eventually appears to return to where it started:

$$
0 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow 7 \rightarrow 0'
$$

But:

$$
\boxed{0' \neq 0}
$$

because the second zero has a **history**.

A piece of clay before and after firing cannot simply be returned to its original condition.

A person after an experience may return to the same place but isn't literally the same previous state.

A breath can leave and another breath enter, but time has passed.

## The simplest statement of the theory

$$
\boxed{\text{A thing is not only what it is. It is also how it became.}}
$$

Or even more simply:

**The numbers show where something is.
The arrows show what happened to it.
The final zero shows that returning is not the same as never having left.**
