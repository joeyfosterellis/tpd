# The Equation, in Joey's words

SOURCED. Two statements by Joey Foster Ellis, quoted verbatim, dated. Nothing here is paraphrased. Later sessions: read this before writing anything about 0 → 7 → 0′.

## Statement 1, 25 September 2026 (afternoon, Kathmandu), sent to Claude Code

> Here's the simplest way I'd explain the equation to someone seeing it cold:
>
> 0 →T1→ 1 →T2→ 2 →T3→ 3 →T4→ 4 →T5→ 5 →T6→ 6 →T7→ 7 →T0→ 0′, with 0′ ≠ 0
>
> **0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.** These are not meanings. They are simply different observable states. "7" is no more special than "3."
>
> **The arrows are the important part.** Each arrow T represents whatever causes one state to become another: heat, breath, pressure, movement, time, language, learning, decay, experience, etc.
>
> **So the theory studies transformation, not the numbers.**
>
> For example: 3 →T4→ 4 asks: What had to happen for state 3 to become state 4?
>
> And: 4 → 3 raises a second question: Can the transformation actually be reversed? Sometimes yes. Sometimes no.
>
> **The final 0′ is the key.** The system eventually appears to return to where it started: 0 → ⋯ → 7 → 0′. But 0′ ≠ 0, because the second zero has a **history**.
>
> A piece of clay before and after firing cannot simply be returned to its original condition. A person after an experience may return to the same place but isn't literally the same previous state. A breath can leave and another breath enter, but time has passed.
>
> So the simplest statement of the theory is: **A thing is not only what it is. It is also how it became.**
>
> Or even more simply: **The numbers show where something is. The arrows show what happened to it. The final zero shows that returning is not the same as never having left.**
