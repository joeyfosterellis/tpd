## 2. The idea, in Joey's words and then in the page's

Joey, 2015, on GIFs and Chinese characters (SOURCED, verbatim):

> It reminds me of Chinese characters. The structure of a Chinese character is made up of other characters (example:). A house + a bird + the sea= Beach. One Chinese character is essentially a condensed, visual paragraph that's concentrated and connected.
>
> A Chinese character is a GIF.

The page's reading (GENERATED): an emoji is a Chinese character that has not been squared off yet. It is a picture of a thing; it is made of parts; and in a row with others it can be read for its sound rather than its thing. The Chinese did this two thousand years ago and called it 假借, borrowing a picture for its sound. A rebus is the same borrowing done for fun.

## 6. Competing readings

1. **A rebus is read for sound, and these are half read for sense.** Bee + leaf works because neither has anything to do with belief. Here 🐂 lends "bull" to a card about the bull's note: the picture and the sound point the same way, which makes it a caption, not a rebus. The strictest rebus on the cards is 🌱 read as "sa" from "seed", and 🎶 read as "core". If Joey wants true rebuses, the emoji should be chosen for their sounds against their senses, and the animals would go.

2. **The Chinese column is school etymology.** 化 as a person and a person upside down, 看 as a hand over an eye: these are the explanations taught to children and repeated in dictionaries; the Shuowen gives some of them and later scholars dispute several. They are here as the same kind of reading the page does with emoji, not as history.

3. **"Conjugation" may mean something else to him.** It might mean the way one emoji changes across the eight states (the peacock at 0 and at 0′), or the way the cards change with the app's after-dark toggle, or grammatical persons for a rebus sentence. Section 4 takes the widest reading and lists every kind of form. If he meant one kind, the others are noise.

### 10.2 On the conjugation: what this record thinks

He meant Unicode's forms. This record agrees that Unicode is the grammar, the fixed set of ways one picture can be inflected: selector, tone, gender, joiner, family. It offers one addition, not a replacement, and did not build it: the page's own tense. The same picture at state 0 and at state 7·0′ is the same codepoint and is not the same emoji, because it has been round the page; 🦚 opens the first card and closes the last. Unicode has no form for that, and the equation is about exactly that. If the states are a conjugation, it is the one Unicode cannot write down, which is the reason to write it here. Whether to put it on the page is his to say.

### 10.3 Competing readings added

4. **First syllables are not sounds.** "Radio" gives "ra" only if the reader hears the first syllable and drops the rest; a strict rebus uses whole-word homophones (saw, knee) and there are not eight of those for the svaras in English. Six of the eight lean on the reader's willingness to cut a word short. The page says so.

5. **The loan characters are the textbook eight.** 我 來 其 萬 北 自 而 西 appear in nearly every introduction to 假借 and their pictorial origins are the received readings, some disputed. As with section 6.2, they are here as the same kind of reading, not as history, and now with a native reader to check them.
